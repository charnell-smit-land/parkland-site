How Do I Know If My Marketing Is Actually Working?

Parkland Consulting Group · November 30, 2026 · 3 min read

The short answer

Your marketing is working when it brings in paying customers, not just likes, clicks or compliments. The simplest test is to ask every new customer how they found you, write it down for a month, and compare what you put into each channel with the customers it actually brought in.

Busy Is Not the Same as Working

Most owners judge marketing by how it feels. A post gets a lot of likes, an ad gets a lot of clicks, someone mentions they saw your flyer. That is attention, and attention is a good start. It only matters if some of those people become customers.

The numbers that are easiest to see, like followers, views and likes, sit furthest from a sale. The number that matters most, new paying customers, usually is not on any dashboard. You have to collect it yourself.

One Question Tells You Most of What You Need

Ask every new customer how they found you. Ask in person, add it to your booking or checkout form, or put it on your intake sheet. Write the answer down every time, even when it is "a friend told me." After a month you will have a plain, honest count of where your customers come from.

Then put that count next to what each channel takes from you, in money and in hours. A channel that eats five hours a week and brings in one customer a month is telling you something. So is the one you barely touch that keeps sending people through the door.

For examplePicture a neighborhood bakery that posts on social media every day and runs a small ad in a local magazine. The posts get plenty of likes, so they feel like the winner. After a month of asking, most new regulars mention the magazine or a friend, and very few mention the posts. The bakery keeps posting, spends less time on it, and puts that time into the ad and a simple referral card.

The Move

  1. Ask Every New CustomerAdd one question, how did you find us, to your checkout, booking form or first conversation. Record every answer for a full month.
  2. Line Up Each ChannelFor each channel, write down what you put in, both money and hours, and how many new customers named it. The Marketing Scorecard below keeps it to one page.
  3. Move Time Toward What WorksShift some time or money from the channel with the weakest return to the one with the strongest. Change one thing at a time, then check again next month.

Pattern Check

  • Do you know how your last 10 new customers found you?
  • Could you say which marketing effort takes the most of your time each week?
  • Have you changed or stopped a marketing effort because of what it brought in, not how it felt?

If any answer was no, one month of asking a single question will tell you more than any follower count.
