How Do I Know Which Customers Are Worth Focusing On?

By Charnell Smith-Landry, Founder · November 4, 2026 · 3 min read

The short answer

In most businesses, a small group of customers brings in a large share of the revenue and the repeat business. Find that group, learn what they have in common, like how they found you and what they bought first, and build more of your business around that path.

Why Serving Everyone the Same Spreads You Thin

Most small businesses treat every customer the same way: the same messages, the same offers, the same follow-up. It feels fair, but it means your best customers get the same attention as people who will never come back.

Your best customers are already showing you what works. They found you a certain way, started with a certain purchase and came back for a reason. When you can see that path, you can send more people down it.

How to Find Them Without Fancy Tools

Export your customer list from whatever you already use, whether that is a booking system, a point of sale, an online store or even invoices. Then sort it three ways: who spent the most, who came back most often and who bought most recently. The names that show up near the top of all three are your core group.

For exampleA hair salon sorted a year of bookings and found that its most loyal clients almost all started with the same service, a consultation and trim. New clients who started with a single color appointment rarely returned. The salon began promoting the consultation first, and repeat bookings grew.

The Move

  1. Sort Your Customer ListRank customers by total spend, number of visits and most recent visit. Mark the people who rank high on all three.
  2. Find What They ShareLook at how those customers first found you, what they bought first and when they came back. Look for the answer that repeats.
  3. Put More Behind That PathPromote the first purchase your best customers made, invest in the channel that brought them and follow up the way they responded to.

Pattern Check

  • Could you name your 10 best customers right now?
  • Do you know how most of them first found you?
  • Do you treat a first-time customer and a ten-time customer differently?

If any answer was no, your customer list is holding a pattern you have not used yet.
